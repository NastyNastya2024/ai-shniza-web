(Using Seedance 2.5, references of character, location & audio [accent recording])

*Audio file not mandatory, but I find it more accurate this way

REFERENCE MAP

•
Image 1 (diner photo) → location reference: booth, red vinyl seats, wood paneling, orange tabletop, condiment caddy, pendant lamps, framed photos on walls. Interior layout only. Ignore its sunny weather, palm trees and exterior street.

•
Image 2 (character reference sheet) → subject/character: face, long voluminous blonde hair, burgundy velvet long-sleeve crop top, dark straight-leg jeans, black belt with gold buckle, layered gold necklaces, gold hoops, black quilted chain bag. Match exactly.

•
Audio 1 (mp3 recording) → Brooklyn accent and dialect reference ONLY. Use it to understand the pronunciation, vowel shapes, rhythm and melody of a heavy Brooklyn accent. Do not use its voice, timbre, pitch, gender or its words. It applies only to the one Brooklyn phrase in Moment 2.

GLOBAL SETUP

•
Year: mid 1990s, New York City diner. Period-accurate set dressing only. No smartphones, no modern screens.

•
Light: daytime, slightly cloudy. Soft diffused window light from frame left, no hard sun patches. Warm pendant lamps glow in the background. Through the window: grey-white sky, brick NYC buildings, out of focus.

•
Format: single continuous take, 7 seconds, no cuts. Handheld camera held by a friend sitting across the booth from her, seated eye level. The camera gently shakes and drifts the entire time and never settles.

•
Framing: medium shot from the tabletop up, filmed from the front. She sits in the left third of the composition, the diner interior fills the right two thirds in soft focus. Background patrons are distant, blurred, and do not react.

•
Performance tone: natural, relaxed, friendly. She talks the way a real person talks to a friend's camera. Understated, no theatrical acting, no exaggerated expressions. Eyes on the lens throughout.

•
Voice: her own natural young adult female voice for every line, including the Brooklyn phrase. Same voice throughout, only the accent changes.

•
Audio: live sync dialogue, natural room tone. Low diner murmur, cutlery and plates. No music.

SECTION 1: EFFECTS TIMELINE

MOMENT 1 (0.0s - 2.0s): Lean In, The Regular Version

•
EFFECT: handheld shake (constant, gentle) + off-center framing (subject frame left) + subject lean-in

•
She leans in toward the lens, forearms sliding onto the table, and starts talking as she moves. Neutral standard American accent, casual and conversational: "She can say, cup of coffee,"

•
The phrase "cup of coffee" here is completely ordinary, clean standard American pronunciation, just a tiny nod on it.

•
Camera wobbles softly, horizon tilting 1-2 degrees.

MOMENT 2 (2.0s - 4.6s): The Brooklyn Version (SIGNATURE MOMENT)

•
EFFECT: handheld shake (constant) + phrase-level accent shift (matched to Audio 1 dialect)

•
She continues in the same breath, neutral accent, easy pace: "or make it..."

•
Then the quoted phrase switches into a thick heavy old Brooklyn dialect, matching the accent heard in Audio 1: "Lemme get a cuppa CAW-fee". "Let me" collapses into "lemme", "cup of" slurs into "cuppa", and the vowel in "coffee" is long, rounded and drawn out, "cawww-fee", slightly nasal, with the sing-song Brooklyn rhythm. Small playful head tilt and a loose hand flick on the phrase.

•
This is the SIGNATURE MOMENT: a plain "cup of coffee" followed by a heavy Brooklyn "lemme get a cuppa caw-fee". The two must sound clearly different. Every word outside this phrase stays neutral American.

MOMENT 3 (4.6s - 5.0s): The Giggle

•
EFFECT: handheld shake (constant) + natural character break

•
Right after "caw-fee" she lets out a very slight, soft giggle at her own accent. It is tiny and breathy, under half a second, more of an amused exhale through a smile than a laugh. Her eyes crinkle, her head dips a few degrees, shoulders give one small bounce. Audible but quiet.

•
The giggle is what breaks her out of the Brooklyn character. She is still smiling as she rolls straight into the next line.

MOMENT 4 (5.0s - 7.0s): It's All In The Prompt

•
EFFECT: handheld shake (constant) + micro push-in (operator leans in, approx 5 percent) + closing gesture

•
Back in her normal neutral accent, relaxed and matter-of-fact, the smile from the giggle still in her voice: "it's all in the prompt."

•
Matching gesture: a small casual shrug, one shoulder lifting as both palms turn up and open just above the table, as if to say "simple as that". The gesture lands on the word "prompt".

•
The smile settles into a slight smirk with eye contact for the last half second. Camera keeps drifting. Clip ends on the smirk, no fade.

SECTION 2: MASTER EFFECTS INVENTORY

1.
Constant gentle handheld shake and drift (used 1x, continuous): documentary texture, never stabilized, never locked off.

2.
Off-center framing, subject frame left (used 1x, continuous): casual friend-filming composition, diner as context at right.

3.
Subject lean-in (used 1x, Moment 1): pulls the viewer in on the first word.

4.
Phrase-level accent shift guided by Audio 1, "cup of coffee" to "lemme get a cuppa caw-fee" (used 1x, Moment 2): the hook of the clip.

5.
Slight giggle character break (used 1x, Moment 3): humanizes the bit and bridges the accent back to her normal voice.

6.
Micro push-in by operator lean (used 1x, Moment 4): small emphasis on the closing line.

7.
Open-palm shrug gesture with held smirk (used 1x, Moment 4): the button.

SECTION 3: EFFECTS DENSITY MAP

0.0s - 2.0s = MEDIUM DENSITY (handheld shake, off-center framing, lean-in: 3 effects in 2.0s)

2.0s - 4.6s = LOW DENSITY (handheld shake, phrase-level accent shift: 2 effects in 2.6s)

4.6s - 5.0s = LOW DENSITY (handheld shake, slight giggle: 2 effects in 0.4s)

5.0s - 7.0s = MEDIUM DENSITY (handheld shake, micro push-in, shrug gesture with smirk: 3 effects in 2.0s)

SECTION 4: MOTION FLOW

•
Opening: she leans in already talking, casual and close.

•
Build: one flowing sentence. The plain phrase comes first, then the Brooklyn version lands with its own rhythm and attitude, and she cracks herself up for a split second.

•
Resolution: still smiling from the giggle, she delivers the plain closing line with an easy shrug. Smirk, eye contact, clip ends while the frame is still moving.

NEGATIVE / CONSTRAINTS

•
Audio 1 is an accent guide only. Do not clone its voice, do not use a male voice, do not reproduce its words or play it in the clip.

•
The Brooklyn dialect applies to "lemme get a cuppa caw-fee" only. The first "cup of coffee", the closing line and all other words are neutral standard American.

•
The giggle is one single, very slight, soft giggle. No full laugh, no loud laughing, no repeated giggling, no covering her mouth.

•
No shouting, no theatrical or exaggerated delivery, no rushed talking, no long dramatic pauses.

•
No pan, no cutaway, she never looks off to the side. No staff entering frame, no background reactions.

•
No cuts, no stabilization, no slow motion, no music, no subtitles or on-screen text.

•
No hard sunlight, no palm trees, no modern objects.

•
Lip sync must match the lines exactly. Character identity, hair and outfit stay consistent with Image 2.

SECTION 1: EFFECTS TIMELINE

Single continuous shot, 5 seconds, no cuts. Real-time speed (100%).

Framing lock for the full clip: tight close-up. Her head and shoulders fill the frame, bottom of frame cuts at upper chest. Never wider than a medium close-up. No waist, legs or feet ever visible. Camera sits at her chest height, about an arm's length away, tilted up roughly 15-20° toward her face. Camera position is an angled profile: about 60-70° off her front, on her left side, so we see a three-quarter profile with the far eye just visible. Shallow depth of field, background from Image 1 reduced to soft color and shapes.

MOMENT 1 (0.0s-1.0s): Open mid-motion

•
EFFECT: Handheld shake (constant) + tight angled-profile close-up + film grain + highlight bloom

•
The clip opens already in motion. She is mid head-turn toward frame right, hair swinging with the turn, a half-smile already forming as she recognizes someone approaching off-frame. No idle beat, no neutral pose, no waiting.

•
Backlight from the street catches the edge of her hair and her gold hoop, blooming softly.

•
Camera is an amateur operator with a 90s consumer camera: irregular micro-jitters, small unmotivated drifts of 2-4° in tilt and roll, a slight over-correction as the operator finds her face. It never locks off.

MOMENT 2 (1.0s-2.6s): The bouquet enters

•
EFFECT: Handheld shake (constant) + partial-subject framing + reactive reframe + focus breathing

•
From frame right and bottom, the man's hand and leather sleeve bring a paper-wrapped bouquet up into frame (pink and yellow flowers matching Image 1). Only the top of the bouquet, his hand and forearm are visible. Petals wobble, paper crinkles.

•
Her eyes drop to the flowers, eyebrows lift, lips part into a surprised smile. Her hands come up from below frame to take the bouquet, shoulders rising slightly. The flower tops stay in the lower edge of frame near her collarbone.

•
Camera reacts late: a small jolt down toward the bouquet, then drifts back up to her face. Focus softens for a fraction of a second, then recovers on her eyes.

MOMENT 3 (2.6s-4.1s): Cheek kiss and reaction. This is the SIGNATURE VISUAL EFFECT

•
EFFECT: Handheld shake (constant) + bloom swell + partial-subject framing + shallow focus hold on her face

•
He leans in from frame right and kisses her far cheek. Because of the angled profile position, he is on the far side of her face: we see only his curly hair, part of his stubbled jaw and the leather collar, partly hidden behind her head. He covers no more than a quarter of the frame. His full face is never visible. Her near-side profile stays completely clear to camera.

•
Her reaction carries the shot: eyes close briefly, a real, slightly shy smile spreads, cheek lifts, head tilts a few degrees into the kiss, a small breathy laugh. Nothing exaggerated, no awareness of the camera.

•
As his head moves in, backlight flares through the gap between their faces. Bloom swells for about half a second, halating around hair edges and her earring, then eases back.

•
Camera pushes in slightly by hand during the kiss, still shaky, with a subtle roll of about 3°.

MOMENT 4 (4.1s-5.0s): Afterglow

•
EFFECT: Handheld shake (constant) + film grain + soft bloom

•
He pulls back out of frame right, leaving only a blur of leather shoulder at the edge. She looks down at the flowers, then glances back toward him off-frame, smile lingering, one hand tucking hair behind her ear.

•
Camera keeps wandering, a small dip and recover as if the operator shifted grip.

•
Clip ends mid-movement, no settle, no hold.

Global look: 90s NYC 35mm film feel, warm Kodak-style color, soft contrast, lifted blacks, fine visible grain, gentle halation on highlights, mild edge softness and slight vignette. Natural daylight only. Natural handheld motion blur. No slow motion, no stabilization, no gimbal smoothness, no look into the lens, no wide or full-body framing at any point.

1.0s-2.6s = MEDIUM DENSITY (handheld shake, partial-subject framing, reactive reframe, focus breathing: 4 effects in 1.6s)

•
Build: All momentum comes from him entering her space. The bouquet rises from the bottom of frame, the camera chases it late, and attention funnels back to her face as he leans in. Bloom swell and her smile peak together around 3.0s-3.7s.
